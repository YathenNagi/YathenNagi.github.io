Based on these results, I would recommend the deal because even if the deal saw no multiple expansion, the sponsor can still realize 22% returns with a 4 year holding period (albeit 19% in 5 years),
and with multiple expansion the returns obviously increase. One risk would be multiple contraction, so would have to assess whether the company is trading at a high multiple to comps already and go from there.
 
2. How do the returns change if you assume Senior Bank Debt of 2x EBITDA or 4x EBITDA instead? Which level is most appropriate?
Returns decrease if the PE firm assumes 2x EBITDA of senior bank debt and increase if they assume 4x. Which level is most appropriate would depend on leverage ratios used in similar, recent deals and
potentially a desired leverage ratio by the PE firm itself (ie if it wants to stay investment grade then it would prefer to only take on 2x of senior bank debt).
 
3. How do the Seller’s Notes affect the returns? What benefits do they provide to the company and the lenders?
The seller's notes increase the returns because (1) they reduce initial upfront investment for PE firm and (2) they charge non-cash interest which provides tax-savings, therefore boosting FCF.
 
4. How does the Shareholder Loan affect the returns compared with traditional Debt or Equity?
The shareholder loan improves returns compared to traditional equity because the non-cash interest paid boosts FCF due to the tax-savings. It also boosts return compared to traditional debt since it repays the principal to itself.
 
5. What is the best way for the PE firm to increase returns in this deal without changing the company’s financial projections?
Increasing leverage, buying at a cheaper multiple/selling at a higher multiple, reducing management rollover/earn-outs (increases sponsor returns not project returns), using more shareholder loans and seller notes as well.
